Experiences

The Antwerp stop, hour by hour, and why

Tap Antwerp on the Route page for the exact hour-by-hour sequence. This is the fuller reasoning behind why it's ordered that way.

Timing

The Cathedral is scheduled last on purpose

Antwerp Cathedral typically doesn't open to visitors until after Sunday mass, roughly 13:00 — going first thing at 09:28 would mean standing outside a closed door. Everything else in the sequence (the station, Grote Markt, brunch, wandering) has no such restriction, so it goes first, and the Cathedral gets the tail end of the window once it's actually open.

Food

Fries are real. Waffles are real, but watch the presentation

Frites are genuine everyday food here, not a tourist invention — double-fried, eaten with mayonnaise or a Belgian sauce like andalouse, not ketchup. Waffles are real too, but there's a difference between the plain Liège waffle a local actually eats walking down the street and the whipped-cream tower sold to tourists near the main square — the first is the real thing. The one genuinely Antwerp-specific item, not generic Belgian, is Antwerpse handjes — a local chocolate/pastry shaped like a hand, tied to the city's founding legend.

Risk

Brunch, not lunch — and it may have a wait

Sunday late morning is peak time for brunch in Belgium, for locals as much as tourists. A good, non-touristy spot without a reservation can run a real 15–30 minute wait. The brunch block is padded to absorb this, but reserving ahead removes the risk entirely rather than hoping the timing works out.

Trade-off

What this costs in Amsterdam that evening

Giving Antwerp 5.5 hours instead of the tighter ~2.5 pulls the Amsterdam evening down to about 5.5 hours too, arriving around 17:13 instead of 14:23. That's enough for a central canal walk and a proper dinner — not enough for Vondelpark, and Begijnhof is off the table regardless, since it closes to visitors before this arrival time either way. Monday and Tuesday already carry Amsterdam's real content (Anne Frank House, Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh, a canal boat tour), which is the reasoning that makes this trade worth it rather than just a loss.

Brussels vs Antwerp

5.5 hours on the ground by default

This is a real toggle on this page, not just a comparison — flip "Brussels instead of Antwerp" above and the route, timeline and bookings all update. Here's the actual reasoning behind the default.

Verdict

Antwerp is the default because the Cathedral is worth real time, not a glance.

At this length, the calculus isn't "which fits a short window better" — it's that Antwerp holds a single genuinely world-class thing, four Rubens altarpieces painted for the exact space they hang in, and that's worth protecting proper time for even though it costs the Amsterdam evening some of its slack. Brussels has more day-to-day depth (more streets, more small sights) but nothing that rises to the same level, so it stays the toggle rather than the default.
